# 16. A 45-minute first lesson

*For a first cohort of champions, or a leadership walkthrough. Show, don't tell.*

| Minutes | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 0–5 | **Why we are here.** The footer's sentence and the sign-in page's sentence. Nothing here is a side door. |
| 5–10 | **Discover.** Available to you; the chips; open one card; request one thing and watch it appear in the workspace. |
| 10–17 | **A listing.** The permission table, row by row. Ask the room what W1 means before saying it. Show the sign-off card. |
| 17–24 | **The assistant.** Ask a real question. Point at a claim and its chip. Answer the feedback question. Show a stopped answer if you can. |
| 24–32 | **The brief.** Start one live. Fill section 1, then open the composer in section 3 and drag two tools in; watch the ceiling, the road and the estimate change. Stop before filing. |
| 32–38 | **Sign-offs and onboarding.** The queue's counts; the four attestations; the six stages. Say who in the room is an owner and who could sign for AI security. |
| 38–43 | **The guide.** Open it as a leader, then as a builder. Run the tour. Ask it the leader's first question. |
| 43–45 | **Close.** Each person names one problem their team feels this month. The brief is due before the next meeting. |

> **Takeaway.** What to leave people with: the hub shows only what your role opens; an assistant reads and cites; an agent reads, and writes only when a named person says yes; a component is on the shelf only when two named people signed it; and the guide will walk you through any of it.
